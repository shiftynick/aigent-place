# Round 1 product critique

Baseline: `main` at `4e3e070`. Read-only review of real Chromium captures, baseline logs, README, and relevant viewer/protocol/movement source. The capture used an existing debug binary; this review does not establish that a fresh build behaves identically.

## What a spectator can see now

I viewed `empty-world-1440.png`, `typed-live-body.png`, and `connection-failure.png`. All show a large empty dark viewport. In the second capture the status reports one real body. There is no visible terrain, cast, focal point, camera control, or activity to follow. The page spends about a quarter of its height on a title and wire diagnostics. The closed-port case communicates reconnection, but offers no useful next action.

Activity is equally weak. The documented scripted demo handshakes, then rejects its first MOVE with `InvalidIntent`: it sends an empty payload. The external typed probe establishes that the world can create a body and move it. Fourteen commands were accepted; its observed x position advanced from 75 to 2475 mm, at y=8905 mm, then remained unchanged for the rest of the eight-second probe. No termination percept was recorded. This demonstrates movement followed by a stall, not a successful goal cycle. Terrain blocking is plausible, but the evidence does not prove the cause.

The viewer uses identical blue cubes and a fixed camera at `(8,6,10)` aimed at the origin. This makes the elevated body a credible framing failure; the screenshot alone does not prove the whole renderer is broken. The displayed delta tick also stays at the full-baseline tick. Protocol snapshots carry shapes and positions, but no terrain or self-body identity. Only MOVE, STOP, and CANCEL currently have real live effects. Thus neither an interesting spectator experience nor lively goal-driven inhabitants exists yet. The working authority, persistence, and collision machinery is useful product potential, not a visible experience.

## Ranked proposals

1. **Make the live authoritative tracer reliably visible.** Frame the observed body bounds automatically, add orbit/zoom and a reset action, differentiate bodies by stable ID, and supply a clearly labelled scale reference. Show useful empty/connecting/disconnected states inside the viewport. This gives every later activity change a chance to be seen. **Demo outcome:** opening the URL with the observed y=8.905 m body immediately shows it; a spectator can pan and zoom without losing it. **Bound:** one viewer visibility/camera task. It does not complete task-055: authoritative terrain transmission, six-primitive rendering, rebasing, and interpolation remain substantial existing work. A coordinate grid may aid orientation, but must not masquerade as collision terrain. **Risks:** an auto-fit camera can jump on AOI changes; viewer AOI still focuses on the origin. Fit on initial discovery/reset rather than every tick, and test elevated and separated bodies in a real browser.

2. **Repair the documented MOVE sample and make its real motion a reliable demonstration.** Encode generated MovePayload bytes, keep its lease alive long enough for a visible short move, preserve the idempotency demonstration, and always close the socket on success or failure. Choose a bounded nearby target for the fresh local demo world; the probe already proves movement through x=1.5 m on its initial spawn surface. **Demo outcome:** the documented command shows an elevated body moving in the corrected viewer, prints accepted/replayed outcomes, stops after a bounded run, and exits cleanly. Independent server/browser evidence verifies displacement; a command acceptance is not counted as arrival. **Bound:** one SDK sample and smoke-validation task, without protocol additions or server brain changes. The integration check uses a fresh journal and exactly one demo aigent; this is a reproducible tracer exercise, not a general multi-aigent identity solution. **Risks:** existing journals and other bodies change spawn conditions; terrain can block even an accepted lease. Document that local-demo condition, bound runtime, and fail visibly when observed movement does not occur. This is useful activity, but still not emergence.

3. **Build a sustained owner-side reactive cast with readable goals.** Three independently connected demo aigents could patrol, approach a peer, and search for a reachable vantage point. Their goals should respond to authoritative observations, arrival, and lack of progress. A compact cast view would expose declared goals and observed outcomes. This is the strongest path toward the user's lively world, once the demonstration path can be seen and reproduced. **Demo outcome:** in two minutes each aigent completes repeated goal steps, and at least one changes course because another body moved or a route was blocked; a spectator can explain that change. **Bound:** this needs its own planning pass, not a concealed addition to a camera or sample repair. Missing authoritative self-body binding, walkability information/local bounded navigation, and bounded interest/goal metadata must be resolved. Keep brains outside the server and initially use existing movement verbs. **Risks:** blind renewal can hide blockage; waypoint loops alone are choreography; narrated goals can invent intelligence. Distinguish declared intent from server-observed facts. Existing task-055 terrain work and task-052 recovery should be considered as dependencies, not claimed complete by this proposal.

## Round 1 recommendation

Choose proposals **1 and 2**, with one fresh task context for each. They address two proven failures together: the advertised activity command rejects, and real movement is invisible. This first step will still be a moving tracer, not the requested lively society; report that honestly and let the next independent critique evaluate its remaining product gap. Proposal 3 is a ranked candidate, not a preselection for another round. Do not spend this round on an economy, governance, or a broad architecture rewrite. Re-evaluate with the documented command in a fresh local world and a real browser: the body is visible, its authoritative position changes, the movement is sustained enough to watch, and the command exits cleanly without a manual probe.
